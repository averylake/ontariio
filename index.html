About the work ↓

01 / The work

The lake holds its form. Everything around it changes.

Across winter, spring, summer, and fall, the surrounding land shifts through colour and flicker. The lake remains the stable element: one continuous body held inside a changing field.

Ontarïio’, Beautiful Lake is both a moving image and an act of record. The animation makes change visible. The mint gives the work a durable public inscription: its title, media, description, and renderer are fixed on-chain.

The blockchain is therefore not simply where the artwork is distributed. It is one of the materials from which the artwork is made.

02 / The record

The inscription is part of the image.

Why the blockchain

The blockchain does not decide what the lake is called. It makes this act of inscription public, dated, signed, and resistant to unilateral revision.

Unlike a webpage, database, or institutional catalogue, the minted inscription has no single custodian who can silently replace the original. Its time, creator signature, and fixed fields can be independently checked across the network. This does not make blockchain an authority over Wendat language, Indigenous history, or the lake itself. It makes the artwork a witness: future interpretations may be added, but this act of naming remains visible beside them.

Ontarïio’ is the Wendat form publicly given by Grand Chief Pierre Picard and confirmed in writing by the Wendat language service for the word carried into the name Ontario: “the lake is beautiful, great.” It is one linguistic lineage among several Indigenous naming traditions connected to this lake. The image itself carries no name. It asks what remains when names and identities are momentarily set aside: the lake remains.

Created and first recorded on Ethereum on 31 August 2026.

The artwork is therefore both image and inscription. The animation visualizes stability; the mint performs it in the public record.
